\begin{abstract}
Standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology \cite{Planck2020} attributes late-time cosmic acceleration to dark energy: a vacuum energy term that fits observations but has no physical explanation.
It requires a 120-order-of-magnitude fine-tuning of vacuum energy \cite{Weinberg1989} and an unexplained coincidence between matter and dark energy densities \cite{Steinhardt1999}.

We present a toy model, the \textbf{External-Node Model}, in which the observable universe is a finite Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) region expanding within a lattice of Hyper-Massive External Attractors (HMEAs).
In this model, cosmic acceleration comes from tidal gravity. As the bubble expands toward neighboring nodes, it is pulled apart. No exotic physics required.

N-body simulations over the late-universe era ($t=5.8 \rightarrow 13.8$ Gyr) reproduce $\Lambda$CDM expansion with \textbf{$R^2 > 0.95$ for expansion rate} and \textbf{$R^2 > 0.99$ for size}, using configurations spanning $M = 92$-$9000 \times M_{obs}$, $S = 15$-$38$ Gpc. Compared directly against the Pantheon$+$SH0ES supernovae, the from-simulation distance--redshift relation fits at \textbf{$\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} \approx 0.44$} --- statistically indistinguishable from $\Lambda$CDM ($0.436$) and decisively rejecting the no-dark-energy Einstein--de~Sitter null ($0.843$) --- and does so for \emph{many} distinct configurations, a match we confirm to be numerically converged up to $10^5$ particles. The physically-motivated realization is a virialized \emph{medium} in equilibrium ($2K/|U|\approx1$) rather than a force-balanced lattice; treating the progenitor node's mass as a single parameter acting in both epochs, a broad and resolution-stable region of the parameter space (68 of 72 progenitor-mass$\,\times\,$realization configurations) is \emph{universe-like}---fitting the supernovae with an anchored expansion, a non-collapsing core, and no node inside the horizon---so the match is generic, not fine-tuned.

The same lattice geometry, with no additional parameters, also produces patterns consistent with three large-scale anomalies: the Hubble Tension ($\Delta H_0/H_0 \approx 5$-$11\%$ \cite{Riess2022}), the CMB ``Axis of Evil'' \cite{Land2005}, and dark flow \cite{Watkins2023}. The model additionally predicts a distinctive phantom-like hyper-acceleration ($w < -1$) as the bubble approaches the surrounding nodes (Section~\ref{sec:phantom}).
We do not attempt to model early-universe dynamics or provide a complete theoretical resolution to these large-scale anomalies; rather, our simulation provides a proof-of-concept that classical gravity is sufficient to reproduce late-time acceleration, implying that tidal forces alone might account for the dynamics we currently label as dark energy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The accelerating expansion of the universe \cite{Riess1998, Perlmutter1999} is conventionally explained by a constant vacuum energy density: the Cosmological Constant ($\Lambda$).
$\Lambda$CDM fits the data well ($\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.7$), but has two serious problems.

The \textbf{Fine-Tuning Problem} \cite{Weinberg1989}: quantum field theory predicts a vacuum energy 120 orders of magnitude larger than observed.
Matching observation requires cancellation to extraordinary precision.

The \textbf{Coincidence Problem} \cite{Steinhardt1999}: dark energy and matter densities happen to be comparable ($\Omega_\Lambda \sim \Omega_m$) in the present epoch. This is a statistically unlikely coincidence with no explanation.

\textbf{We propose a different interpretation.} Rather than assuming the FRW metric describes all of spacetime, we model the observable universe as a finite FRW region expanding within a larger space containing discrete massive objects.
In this picture, ``dark energy'' is a misidentification: the \textbf{intrinsic repulsion} attributed to the vacuum is actually \textbf{extrinsic attraction} from a surrounding mass distribution.
We call this the \textbf{External-Node Model}.
This resolves the fine-tuning problem directly: gravity is weak at large scales, so the required forces are small.
A companion \textbf{Progenitor Hypothesis} (Section~\ref{sec:progenitor}) addresses isotropy. In this hypothesis, the Big Bang would have originated from a destabilized node within the meta-structure, inheriting the symmetry of its equilibrium position.
Together, the External-Node mechanism and the Progenitor Hypothesis constitute what we term the \textbf{Progenitor Node Model}.

The idea that trans-observable structure could affect cosmological dynamics is not new.
Mersini-Houghton and Holman \cite{MersiniHoughton2009} proposed that entanglement with neighboring landscape domains could produce bulk flows and CMB anomalies; others have noted that matter beyond the particle horizon could source coherent motions \cite{Kashlinsky2008}.
But prior proposals address individual anomalies with purpose-built mechanisms.
Our framework is different: a single lattice geometry, motivated by the need to reproduce cosmic acceleration, naturally yields patterns consistent with the Hubble Tension, the CMB ``Axis of Evil,'' and dark flow (Section~\ref{sec:predictions}) as an immediate consequence, with no additional parameters. This unification under one geometric structure is, to our knowledge, novel.

\section{The Physical Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{The Virialized Grid Topology}
\label{sec:grid}
Standard cosmology assumes the Cosmological Principle (homogeneity and isotropy) holds at all scales.
We propose that it holds only within our local bubble.
Beyond our horizon, we model a \textbf{Virialized Meta-Structure}: a self-gravitating arrangement of mass concentrations in \emph{virial equilibrium}, stable over vast timescales but irregular in detail—much like galaxies within a relaxed cluster.

\paragraph{What ``virialized'' must mean here.} It is worth being precise, because a natural first guess is wrong. Virial equilibrium is \emph{not} a static force balance: a virialized system is supported by velocity dispersion, $2K/|U| \approx 1$, and its members orbit and feel non-zero forces at every instant. The only structures with static, everywhere-vanishing net force are crystals and glasses. We accordingly model the meta-structure as an \textbf{infinite, statistically homogeneous, self-gravitating medium at virial equilibrium}---realized numerically as a periodic box (an infinite tiling, so the mean-density background force cancels by symmetry as it must in an infinite medium, which a finite chunk cannot reproduce) relaxed to $2K/|U|\approx1$, with a softening of order the node spacing to keep it collisionless. Such a medium is homogeneous when coarse-grained but disordered and locally clumpy: clusters and voids, not a lattice.

This distinction has teeth. An earlier version of this work used a symmetric \emph{lattice}, which appeared to be exactly force-balanced at its centre; that cancellation turned out to be an artefact of assigning the central site a full node's mass. The physical central object is the progenitor---our own observable universe's precursor (Section~\ref{sec:progenitor})---whose mass is a small fraction of a node's, and which therefore cannot cancel the surrounding field. We keep the ordered lattice only as an idealization for the analytic estimates of Section~\ref{sec:tidal}, and use the virialized medium for the physical results of Section~\ref{sec:hires}.

These concentrations, termed \textbf{Hyper-Massive External Attractors (HMEAs)}, are compact objects far older than 13.8 Gyr, with masses $M_{ext} \gg M_{obs}$, separated by voids much larger than the observable universe.
Our universe sits in one such void, expanding toward the surrounding nodes.

Figure~\ref{fig:meta-structure} shows this local arrangement: the HMEA nodes in our neighbourhood, sized and coloured by mass, with our observable universe drawn to scale. Because a node's tidal pull falls as $1/d^3$ with distance (Section~\ref{sec:tidal}), the nearby nodes dominate the dynamics; the meta-structure's mass segregation (more massive nodes further out, as in a relaxed cluster) must therefore stay mild enough that these near nodes retain masses of order $M_{ext}$.

\subsection{The Progenitor Hypothesis}
\label{sec:progenitor}
A standard objection to bubble cosmologies: why is our universe at the center of the void, making expansion appear isotropic?

The \textbf{Progenitor Hypothesis} answers this.
The Big Bang was not a creation event \textit{ex nihilo} in random empty space, but the destabilization of a specific object:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Origin}: Our universe originates from a \textbf{Progenitor Node} located at the gravitational equilibrium point between its neighbors, which was formerly a member of the Virialized Grid.
\item \textbf{Event}: The Progenitor Node destabilized (via critical mass accumulation, quantum instability, or collision) and transitioned from a stable object into a rapidly expanding cloud of matter and radiation.
\item \textbf{Isotropy}: Because the Progenitor Node was positioned at the equilibrium point, its subsequent expansion proceeded symmetrically into the potential wells of the surrounding structure, naturally accounting for isotropy without fine-tuning.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{One mass, two epochs.} The hypothesis carries a quantitative consequence that is easy to miss: the progenitor's mass $M_{prog}$ is a \emph{single} physical parameter that must act in \emph{both} epochs. Before the Big Bang it is the mass of one member of the virialized medium---it participates in the equilibrium and shapes the local arrangement of its neighbours. After the destabilization it is the mass of everything that came out, i.e. our observable universe \emph{plus} whatever matter now lies beyond our horizon. We therefore do not treat the two as independent: in the simulations of Section~\ref{sec:hires}, one parameter sets the progenitor's mass in the pre-collapse medium and the corresponding matter budget outside the observable region afterwards.

The theoretically distinguished value is $M_{prog} = M_{ext}$: the progenitor was a \emph{typical} node of the meta-structure, neither special nor selected. We test the parameter over four orders of magnitude around this point and find (Section~\ref{sec:universe-like}) that the typical-node value is also the structurally best-behaved---a convergence between what the hypothesis prefers a priori and what the simulations single out a posteriori.

\section{Dynamics and Quantitative Analysis}
\label{sec:dynamics}
Expansion is governed by two competing forces: internal self-gravity (deceleration) and external tidal gravity (acceleration), with the latter acting as the physical mechanism replacing dark energy.

\subsection{The Tidal Acceleration Mechanism}
\label{sec:tidal}
Under the standard shell theorem, a continuous spherical external distribution exerts no net force, meaning deceleration is governed entirely by the enclosed mass $M_{int}$. Our lattice geometry alters this dynamic: because the external mass is concentrated in discrete nodes rather than a smooth shell, it generates a net tidal pull.

To model this effect in three dimensions using the simplest possible symmetric geometry, we arrange the external mass concentrations into a $3\times3\times3$ lattice containing 26 nodes. While the real-world distribution would be far more irregular, this idealized lattice allows us to isolate the core physics. At the exact center of this grid ($R=0$), the forces from the surrounding nodes perfectly cancel. However, as our expanding universe moves outward, it climbs the potential gradient of the nearest HMEA, and the resulting tidal force increases with distance from the center.

Evaluating this tidal contribution along a single axis toward the nearest node, the acceleration from an external mass $M_{ext}$ at a grid distance $S$ is given by:

\begin{equation}
a_{tidal} \approx \frac{G M_{ext}}{(S - R)^2} - \frac{G M_{ext}}{S^2}
\label{eq:tidal-exact}
\end{equation}

For $R \ll S$, Taylor expansion gives a linear dependence on $R$:

\begin{equation}
a_{tidal} \approx \frac{2 G M_{ext}}{S^3} R
\label{eq:tidal-linear}
\end{equation}

While this single-axis derivation isolates the nearest node, it captures the correct linear-in-$R$ scaling and provides a sound order-of-magnitude estimate of the tidal field. At the central equilibrium point ($R = 0$), all forces cancel. As the universe expands, tidal forces scale as $\sim G M / d^3$, so the nearest face node at distance $S$ dominates over edge nodes at $S\sqrt{2}$ ($\sim 35\%$ contribution) and corner nodes at $S\sqrt{3}$ ($\sim 19\%$). The full 26-node system is accounted for in our numerical simulation (Section~\ref{sec:numerical}).

The critical result here is the linear $R$ dependence. In the standard Friedmann equations, the cosmological constant drives an acceleration of $H_0^2 \Omega_\Lambda R$. Consequently, in the non-relativistic limit, this single-axis external tidal field is mathematically indistinguishable from $\Lambda$ \emph{along the axis to a node}. The following subsection sharpens this statement: the \emph{full} three-dimensional symmetric field is traceless, so the indistinguishability holds for the axial magnitude but not for the isotropic volume term.

\subsection{Traceless Tides: a Correction to the Isotropy Claim}
\label{sec:traceless}
The single-axis derivation above (Equation~\ref{eq:tidal-linear}) is correct as the radial acceleration \emph{toward the nearest node}, but it must not be read as an isotropic, $\Lambda$-like volume term. In the vacuum between the nodes the external potential satisfies Laplace's equation, $\nabla^2 \Phi_{ext} = \sum_{\text{nodes}} \nabla^2(-GM_{ext}/r) = 0$ away from the sources. Therefore the external tidal tensor $T_{ij} = -\partial_i \partial_j \Phi_{ext}$ is \textbf{traceless}: $\mathrm{Tr}\,T = -\nabla^2 \Phi_{ext} = 0$. A traceless tensor cannot drive net isotropic expansion to linear order in $R/S$; it can only produce \textbf{shear}.

Concretely, for the symmetric equal-mass lattice the tidal tensor at the exact centre \emph{vanishes} by symmetry. Making one face heavier (or one axis tighter) yields a tensor of the form
\begin{equation}
T \;\sim\; \frac{G M_{ext}}{S^3}\,\mathrm{diag}(+4,\,-2,\,-2),
\label{eq:traceless-shear}
\end{equation}
which is manifestly traceless: a pure stretch along one axis with compensating compression along the other two, plus a net dipole. There is no $\mathrm{diag}(+1,+1,+1)$ isotropic piece available at this order.

The model's match to the \emph{isotropic} root-mean-square expansion history is therefore \textbf{not} the linear tidal term. It arises from two genuinely present but distinct effects: (i) the \textbf{nonlinear} $(S-R)^{-2}$ growth of the force as particles move off-centre toward the nodes (Equation~\ref{eq:tidal-exact}, beyond the linear Taylor term), and (ii) the calibrated initial Hubble-flow velocities (Section~\ref{sec:framework}). A direct consequence, confirmed numerically (Section~\ref{sec:from-data}), is that the isotropic supernova Hubble diagram \emph{cannot} discriminate the lattice configuration: the reduced $\chi^2$ is essentially flat in $M$ once each $M$ is paired with its growth-anchored $S$, because the data constrain only the effective expansion ($\sim$\,effective $\Omega_\Lambda$), i.e. the ratio $M/S^3$. This is the quantitative footprint of the $M/S^3$ degeneracy noted in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

This correction \emph{sharpens} the model rather than retracting it. The isotropic background is reproduced, but the model's real, distinguishing signal lives in the \textbf{anisotropy} it predicts---a shear and an associated Hubble dipole---which the per-node HMEA masses directly control (Sections~\ref{sec:dipole}--\ref{sec:dark-flow}). This is exactly the regime the standard $\Lambda$ term cannot mimic, and it is where the model is genuinely falsifiable.

\subsection{Estimation of Required Node Mass and Distance}
\label{sec:estimation}
Equating the tidal acceleration (Equation~\ref{eq:tidal-linear}) to the observed dark energy acceleration:

\begin{equation}
H_0^2 \Omega_\Lambda \approx \frac{2 G M_{ext}}{S^3}
\label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}

Solving for the grid spacing $S$:

\begin{equation}
S \approx \left( \frac{2 G M_{ext}}{H_0^2 \Omega_\Lambda} \right)^{1/3}
\end{equation}

Assuming $M_{ext} = 5 \times 10^{55} \text{ kg}$ ($\approx 500 \times M_{obs}$, where $M_{obs} \approx 10^{53} \text{ kg}$), $H_0 \approx 70 \text{ km/s/Mpc} \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-18} \text{ s}^{-1}$, and $\Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.7$:

\begin{equation}
S \approx \left( \frac{2 \times 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \cdot 5 \times 10^{55}}{3.7 \times 10^{-36}} \right)^{1/3} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{27} \text{ meters}
\end{equation}

Converting to parsecs ($1 \text{ Mpc} \approx 3.08 \times 10^{22} \text{ m}$):

\begin{equation}
S \approx 39 \text{ Gigaparsecs (Gpc)}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Result:} Nodes of $\sim 10^{55}$ kg spaced $\sim 40$ Gpc apart provide the gravitational acceleration needed to reproduce $\Lambda$.
The nodes sit well beyond the observable universe.
Their electromagnetic silence is expected—Section~\ref{sec:defenses} argues they are ancient black holes that exhausted their local fuel supply long ago.

\paragraph{Analytical vs. Numerical Range:}
Equation~\ref{eq:matching} predicts $S \approx 39$ Gpc for $M_{ext} \approx 500 \times M_{obs}$.
Numerical exploration finds viable configurations across $S = 15$-$70$ Gpc, with balanced-optimization results clustering at $S = 15$-$38$ Gpc. This is a broad range, not a fine-tuned point.

\section{Numerical Validation}
\label{sec:numerical}

\subsection{N-Body Simulation Framework}
\label{sec:framework}
We validate the mechanism with an N-body simulation: tracer particles expanding under internal self-gravity and external tidal forces from the HMEA grid.

\paragraph{Grid Configuration:}
We use the same 3$\times$3$\times$3 lattice introduced in Section~\ref{sec:tidal}: 26 external nodes with our universe at the central vacancy, and HMEAs at integer multiples of the grid spacing $S$.
Distant nodes contribute negligibly to the tidal force, so this lattice is sufficient.

\paragraph{Simulation Parameters:}
2000 particles, 8 Gyr ($t = 5.8 \rightarrow 13.8$ Gyr), covering the observed acceleration era.
We start at $t = 5.8$ Gyr because the model targets late-time acceleration only (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
Initial conditions match $\Lambda$CDM at $t = 5.8$ Gyr.

\paragraph{Parameter Exploration:}
Systematic sweeps over $(M_{ext}, S)$ using adaptive linear search.
The $\Lambda$CDM baseline is computed analytically via exact Friedmann solution at each snapshot time.
Quality checks: matter-only never exceeds $\Lambda$CDM at any timestep, center-of-mass drift is monitored, and runaway particles are flagged.

\paragraph{Fit Metric:}
Agreement is quantified using $R^2$ (coefficient of determination):
\[
R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_{model} - y_{\Lambda CDM})^2}{\sum (y_{\Lambda CDM} - \bar{y}_{\Lambda CDM})^2}
\]
computed for both size evolution and expansion rate over the full 8 Gyr period.

\paragraph{Numerical Robustness:}
We report the worst-performing random seed to guard against a fortuitously favorable seed.
$R^2$ metrics converge above $N \sim 2000$; below $\sim$500 particles, individual particles can represent too large a mass fraction, producing ejection artifacts from close encounters.
Above this threshold, expansion dynamics are mostly insensitive to particle count.

\subsection{Optimization Tradeoffs: Model Accuracy and Expansion Dynamics}
\label{sec:tradeoffs}

Evaluating the model's performance requires balancing two distinct metrics: the fit to the cumulative expansion radius, $R(t)$, and the fit to the expansion rate, $H(t)$. Because the expansion rate is the first derivative of the radius, it imposes a more stringent constraint on the underlying dynamics. 

A ``size-only'' optimization can achieve a near-perfect fit to the final radius (e.g., $M=800 M_{obs}, S=22$ Gpc, $R^2_{size} = 0.9991$), yet fail to reproduce the correct expansion history, as the path integrates to the same displacement despite different local dynamics. By contrast, a balanced approach yields $R^2_{size} > 0.99$ and $R^2_{rate} > 0.95$, significantly outperforming the matter-only baseline ($R^2_{rate} \approx 0.84$). 

Two factors limit these rate comparisons. First, we compare our model's isotropic root-mean-square (RMS) expansion to the Friedmann $H(t)$, but the framework inherently predicts anisotropic expansion (Section~\ref{sec:dipole}); some discrepancy likely arises from this geometric effect. Second, the current Hubble tension ($\sim 8.6\%$) introduces inherent uncertainty into the ``target'' $H(t)$ of $\Lambda$CDM. 

\paragraph{Primary Results.}
Our primary configuration ($M_{ext} = 9000 M_{obs}$, $S = 38$ Gpc; Figure~\ref{fig:primary}) successfully tracks $\Lambda$CDM to within 2\% in total size ($R^2_{size} = 0.9954$) and matches the expansion history ($R^2_{rate} = 0.9630$), yielding an intrinsic $H_0 \approx 70$ km/s/Mpc without post-hoc adjustment.

\subsection{Comparison to Real Supernova Data}
\label{sec:from-data}

The $R^2$ results above compare the simulated expansion to the \emph{analytic} $\Lambda$CDM curve---a model-versus-theory test. As a stronger, non-circular check we now compare the simulation directly to \textbf{real data}: the Pantheon$+$SH0ES Type~Ia supernova compilation \cite{Brout2022}.

\paragraph{Method (non-circular).}
We run the External-Node dynamics from $t = 2.9$ Gyr to today (covering the observed range $z \lesssim 2.1$) and build the distance modulus directly from the simulated scale factor,
\begin{equation}
\mu(z) = 5\log_{10}\!\left[\frac{(1+z)\,c\!\int_{t}^{t_0}\! dt'/a(t')}{10\,\mathrm{pc}}\right],
\label{eq:mu-from-sim}
\end{equation}
integrating the \emph{actual} $a(t)$ the particles produce (internal gravity, the tidal nodes, Hubble drag). This is \textbf{not} the analytic $\Omega_{\Lambda,eff}$ shortcut, which reproduces $\Lambda$CDM by construction and cannot fail; Equation~\ref{eq:mu-from-sim} reflects whatever the dynamics did. We clip to the covered redshift range, marginalize each model's additive magnitude offset, and report the reduced $\chi^2$ against the full Pantheon$+$ covariance. Because $\mu(z)$ is invariant under a rescaling of $a$, we additionally impose a physical \textbf{growth anchor}---the total expansion must match the $\Lambda$CDM background ($a(t_0)/a(t_{\text{start}}) \approx 3.30$ at $t_{\text{start}} = 2.9$ Gyr) within $20\%$---so the separation from the no-dark-energy null is physical, not an artefact of free time-rescaling.

\paragraph{Result.}
The from-simulation fit reaches $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} \approx 0.44$---statistically indistinguishable from analytic $\Lambda$CDM ($0.437$)---and sits far from the Einstein--de~Sitter no-dark-energy null ($0.844$), which the data decisively reject (Table~\ref{tab:pantheon}, Figure~\ref{fig:pantheon}). This headline uses the model's realistic form: a structured node distribution with a wide mass function, scored at the \textbf{best-matching observer} (we are not at the cloud's centre). It is reproduced by \emph{many} configurations and is numerically converged to $10^5$ particles; Section~\ref{sec:hires} details how it is obtained and why a good vantage is generic. A deliberately conservative baseline---uniform node masses read at the central observer---gives $0.501$, still \emph{with} $\Lambda$CDM and far from the null.

\paragraph{Interpretation (stated plainly).}
Three honest conclusions follow. \textbf{The model produces effective dark energy}: the from-simulation curve sits \emph{with} $\Lambda$CDM and \emph{far} from the Einstein--de~Sitter null, so the tidal mechanism supplies what the data require. \textbf{It matches but does not beat $\Lambda$CDM}: at $\approx 0.44$ versus $0.437$ the two are indistinguishable at observable redshifts. \textbf{It is degenerate in $M/S^3$}: the isotropic Hubble diagram constrains only the effective expansion rate (Section~\ref{sec:traceless}), so the supernovae do not pin $M$ and $S$ separately. We do not read $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} < 1$ as an exceptional fit---it reflects the conservative Pantheon$+$ covariance; the informative quantities are the \emph{separations}. The signal that could distinguish this model from $\Lambda$ lives not in the isotropic Hubble diagram but in the predicted anisotropy (Sections~\ref{sec:dipole}--\ref{sec:dark-flow}).

\subsection{High-resolution refinement: virialized structure and the observer-dependent fit}
\label{sec:hires}

The $0.501$ result above uses a \emph{uniform}-mass node shell and the cloud's central observer. Two physically-motivated refinements sharpen it. First, we replace the uniform shell with a \textbf{structured node distribution carrying a log-normal mass function}, modelling a relaxed meta-structure rather than equal-mass points; a spread in node masses avoids the small-$S$ cloud collapse and reaches the genuinely accelerating regime. Second, and more importantly, we drop the assumption that we sit at the cloud's centre. The probability of occupying the exact centre is negligible, and the model predicts a \emph{position-dependent} expansion history; the supernova data localise us to whichever vantage they match. We therefore treat every particle as a candidate observer (its $\mu(z)$ built from the local growth of its own neighbourhood) and report the \textbf{best-matching observer}, alongside the \emph{fraction} of vantages below each reference so the genericity of a good vantage stays visible.

We report results for two node realizations: the ordered \textbf{lattice} used in the scans below, and the physically-motivated \textbf{virialized medium} of Section~\ref{sec:grid}, which supersedes it as the structural model (Section~\ref{sec:universe-like}). The supernova fit is essentially the same in both---$\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}\approx0.44$---which is itself informative: the isotropic Hubble diagram is insensitive to the structural difference, exactly as the traceless-tide argument of Section~\ref{sec:traceless} predicts. The structure is discriminated instead by the internal criteria of Section~\ref{sec:universe-like}.

With these refinements the from-simulation fit improves to $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} \approx 0.44$ --- essentially $\Lambda$CDM ($0.436$) --- and, crucially, this is reached by \textbf{many distinct configurations}: a fine scan over $M$, $S$, and the mass-function width all land on Pantheon$+$ at $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} \approx 0.44$--$0.45$ and far above the Einstein--de~Sitter null (Figure~\ref{fig:hires-multiplicity}). A Pantheon-matching vantage is thus a generic, not a fine-tuned, feature of the model. Figure~\ref{fig:hires-cloud} shows one such configuration evolved at high resolution.

\subsection{Beyond the Hubble diagram: which configurations are ``universe-like''?}
\label{sec:universe-like}

The supernova fit alone is a weak discriminator. Because the external tidal field is traceless (Section~\ref{sec:traceless}), the isotropic Hubble diagram constrains only the effective expansion rate, so wide swathes of parameter space fit it equally well---including configurations whose internal structure is plainly unphysical. Selecting on $\chi^2$ alone can therefore actively pick the \emph{wrong} configuration; we found cases where the best-fitting choice of node spacing was one whose observable region contained a collapsing core.

We therefore adopt a \textbf{composite criterion}. A configuration is \emph{universe-like} only if it simultaneously satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Supernova fit}: best-observer $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} \le 0.46$ against Pantheon$+$SH0ES;
\item \textbf{Anchored expansion}: total growth within $20\%$ of the $\Lambda$CDM background (Section~\ref{sec:from-data});
\item \textbf{No runaway collapse}: the observable region's central core must not be collapsing---we trace the particles that end up in the core back to their initial radii and require the ratio to be $\ge 0.9$ (i.e. static or expanding);
\item \textbf{Empty horizon}: no HMEA within the observable radius ($\approx 14$ Gpc), since we observe none.
\end{enumerate}
Criterion (3) is the sharpest addition. The tidal field is compressive along two of its three axes (Equation~\ref{eq:traceless-shear}), and at close node spacings this compression can decouple a Gpc-scale overdensity from the expansion entirely---a structure the real universe does not contain. We verified that this collapse is genuine physics and not a numerical artefact: it is unchanged when the tree-based force solver is replaced by exact direct summation, and it persists across independent realizations of the initial conditions.

\paragraph{The progenitor mass controls the core.}
We scanned the progenitor mass $M_{prog}$ (Section~\ref{sec:progenitor}) over four orders of magnitude against eight independent realizations of the medium, with the node spacing co-fitted per configuration (Figure~\ref{fig:centerm-knot}). The clearest statement we can defend is a negative one paired with a narrow positive.

The negative: \textbf{there is no clean transition in $M_{prog}$}. Collapsing and expanding cores coexist throughout the intermediate decades---the realization matters as much as the progenitor mass---so a scan over a single realization would badly overstate any trend, and we do not claim that a heavier progenitor monotonically cures the core. The positive is narrower but more interesting: at the theoretically distinguished value $M_{prog}=M_{ext}$, the realizations cluster tightly at or just above the stability threshold ($0.90$--$1.07$), the tightest spread anywhere in the scan, with no strong collapse or runaway expansion in any realization tested. The lowest masses, by contrast, collapse unambiguously. The value the Progenitor Hypothesis prefers a priori is thus also the most \emph{realization-robust} a posteriori.

One further caveat is important, because it identifies the real mechanism. The direct lever on the core is not $M_{prog}$ but the \emph{node spacing} $S$: re-running a collapsing configuration at a larger fixed $S$ un-collapses it, at a fit cost of only $\sim0.01$--$0.03$ in $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$. Much of the apparent progenitor-mass pattern is therefore mediated by which $S$ the fit happens to select per configuration. This is precisely why we select $S$ jointly on fit \emph{and} core stability rather than on $\chi^2$ alone.

\paragraph{Universe-like configurations are generic.}
With that joint selection, the parameter space is broadly viable: \textbf{68 of 72} progenitor-mass $\times$ realization configurations satisfy all four criteria (Table~\ref{tab:universe-like}), against 43 of 71 when the spacing is chosen on $\chi^2$ alone. The qualifying configurations span the full four decades of $M_{prog}$ and all eight realizations, with best-observer $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}$ clustered at $0.433$--$0.44$ and nearest nodes at $55$--$140$ Gpc, far outside the horizon. Being universe-like is thus a \emph{generic} property of the model, not a fine-tuned corner---the same multiplicity argument as Figure~\ref{fig:hires-multiplicity}, now under a criterion that also constrains internal structure. The four failures are informative rather than fatal: one is a genuine runaway, one hits the edge of the searched spacing range, and two are low-$M_{prog}$ configurations whose cores collapse.

\paragraph{Resolution stability.}
We re-ran the entire grid at fourfold particle count ($8\times10^3$), with the node spacing re-fitted from scratch in each configuration. The verdict is stable: \textbf{68 of 72} configurations remain universe-like, against $69$ at the lower resolution, and the fitted spacings shift by a median of only $1$ Gpc. Only three of the seventy-two configurations change status, and all three sit at the core-stability boundary---one is rescued, and two low-$M_{prog}$ configurations drop out as their cores, marginally stable at low resolution, are resolved as genuinely collapsing. This is the expected direction: higher resolution resolves compression that coarser sampling smooths over. It also sharpens the physical claim---core collapse at low progenitor mass is a real, resolution-converged feature of the model, and the heavy-progenitor configurations that the Progenitor Hypothesis prefers are precisely the ones unaffected by it.

We caution against over-reading the absolute fit values across resolutions. The best-observer statistic depends on how densely the candidate vantages are sampled, and it shifts by $\pm0.005$--$0.01$ between resolutions in \emph{both} directions; the robust, resolution-stable claim is the \emph{qualification} (Table~\ref{tab:universe-like}), not a ranking against $\Lambda$CDM at the third decimal place.

\section{Scope and Limitations}
\label{sec:scope}
\label{sec:limitations}

This is a toy model, not a complete cosmological theory.
What it does not address:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{General Relativity}: We use Newtonian gravity in flat Euclidean space. The tidal field is modeled as a pre-existing condition of the meta-structure, not as a force propagating from current node positions; nevertheless, a full GR treatment is needed to confirm the mechanism at these scales.
\item \textbf{Early Universe}: No treatment of inflation, baryogenesis, or nucleosynthesis. The Progenitor destabilization is posited, not derived.
\item \textbf{CMB Power Spectrum}: Effects on acoustic oscillations and Planck constraints are not computed \cite{Planck2020}.
\item \textbf{BAO}: Standard ruler constraints \cite{Eisenstein2005} have not been tested against this model.
\end{itemize}

\noindent A full theory would require relativistic treatment of the meta-structure's metric, tidal forces in curved spacetime, and a proper domain boundary for the Cosmological Principle.

We do not claim this model represents physical reality, but that classical alternatives to vacuum energy are viable and worth investigating.

\section{Predictions and Falsifiability}
\label{sec:predictions}
The model makes three distinct predictions, plus one qualitative consistency, all from the same lattice geometry.
Because the meta-structure is irregular (varying node masses and positions), it imprints angular signatures—dipole, quadrupole, octopole—sharing a common preferred direction.
Confirming or ruling out any one constrains the others.

\subsection{The Geometric ``Big Rip'' (Hyper-Acceleration)}
\label{sec:phantom}
$\Lambda$CDM predicts constant dark energy density and smooth exponential expansion ($w = -1$) forever.
The HMEA model predicts acceleration that strengthens over time.

\paragraph{Prediction:} As $R(t) \rightarrow S$, the tidal force scales as $(S-R)^{-2}$—acceleration increases as the universe approaches the nodes.

\paragraph{Observable:} The effective equation of state $w$ should drift below $-1$ (phantom energy \cite{Caldwell2003}), detectable via precision $w(z)$ measurements at low redshift.

\paragraph{Quantitative Analysis:}
The total effective equation of state $w_{eff} = -1 - \frac{2}{3}\frac{d \ln H}{d \ln a}$ characterizes the net expansion behavior.
For M=9000$\times$M$_{obs}$, S=38 Gpc, the current $R/S \approx 0.19$—well within the linear regime.
Phantom deviation grows as $R/S \rightarrow 1$.
Configurations with smaller $S$ (e.g., S=15 Gpc, $R/S \approx 0.48$) would show stronger present-day phantom signatures.

\subsection{Dipole Anisotropy}
\label{sec:dipole}
The Progenitor Hypothesis gives a roughly isotropic start, but the irregular meta-grid means the nearest HMEAs have different masses and distances.

\paragraph{Prediction:} Surveys (Euclid \cite{Euclid2011}, LSST \cite{LSST2019}) should detect a \textbf{dipole in the expansion rate}—one hemisphere expanding slightly faster than the other, toward the nearest or most massive HMEA.
This could contribute to the Hubble Tension: the $\sim$8.6\% discrepancy between CMB-calibrated ($\sim$67 km/s/Mpc) and distance-ladder ($\sim$73 km/s/Mpc) values of $H_0$ \cite{Riess2022, DiValentino2021}.
CMB-derived $H_0$ is an all-sky average; distance-ladder measurements sample specific directions.
A $\sim$5-11\% dipole could bias the latter relative to the former, producing an apparent method-dependent discrepancy.

\paragraph{Quantitative Estimate:}
Assuming 5\% position irregularity and 20\% mass variation (conservative for a virialized structure), with $\Omega_{\Lambda,eff} \equiv 2 G M_{ext} / (S^3 H_0^2)$, the tidal asymmetry propagates to $H_0$ via:

\[
\frac{\Delta H_0}{H_0} \approx \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\Omega_{\Lambda,eff}}{\Omega_m + \Omega_{\Lambda,eff}} \cdot \sqrt{\left(\frac{2\delta_{eff}}{S - R}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\Delta M_{eff}}{M}\right)^2}
\]

where $\delta_{eff} = \delta_{pos}/\sqrt{6}$ and $\Delta M_{eff} = \Delta M / \sqrt{6}$ account for statistical cancellation across 6 face nodes. Position and mass variations add in quadrature.
For M=9000$\times$M$_{obs}$, S=38 Gpc, $R \approx 7.25$ Gpc:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Position irregularity only (5\%): $\Delta H_0 / H_0 \approx 2.4\%$ (grid average)
    \item Mass variation only (20\%): $\Delta H_0 / H_0 \approx 3.9\%$ (grid average)
    \item \textbf{Combined (position + mass)}: $\Delta H_0 / H_0 \approx 4.6\%$ ($\sim$3.2 km/s/Mpc, grid average)
    \item \textbf{Single nearest node (worst case)}: $\Delta H_0 / H_0 \approx 11.3\%$ ($\sim$7.9 km/s/Mpc)
    \item Observed Hubble Tension: $\sim$8.6\% (6 km/s/Mpc difference between 67 and 73 km/s/Mpc)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Implications:}
The predicted dipole ($\sim$4.6\% grid average, up to $\sim$11.3\% worst case) brackets the Hubble Tension ($\sim$8.6\%).
Detection of a $\sim$5-11\% $H_0$ dipole uncorrelated with local structure would support the model; absence of any dipole above $\sim$2\% would constrain it.

\subsection{CMB Large-Angle Anomalies (The ``Axis of Evil'')}
\label{sec:axis-of-evil}

The CMB quadrupole ($l=2$) and octopole ($l=3$) share a preferred axis at a level inconsistent with isotropy ($p < 0.5\%$) \cite{Land2005, deOliveiraCosta2004, Copi2006}.
$\Lambda$CDM offers no explanation.

\paragraph{Prediction:}
The HMEA lattice has nodes at three distinct distances: 6 face nodes at $S$, 12 edge at $S\sqrt{2}$, 8 corner at $S\sqrt{3}$.
This discrete geometry imprints angular modes on the tidal field:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dipole} ($l=1$): nearest node closer or more massive than opposing node (Section~\ref{sec:dipole}).
    \item \textbf{Quadrupole} ($l=2$): face-node asymmetries along the three principal axes.
    \item \textbf{Octopole} ($l=3$): corner-node tetrahedral sub-symmetry.
\end{itemize}

All multipoles come from the same lattice, so their axes are necessarily correlated—matching the observed alignment.
The Progenitor Hypothesis reinforces this: expanding matter inherits the tidal imprint from the start.
The correlation with the ecliptic plane is likely coincidental.

\paragraph{Qualitative Comparison:}
Observed anomalies \cite{Copi2010}: (1) low quadrupole power, (2) planar octopole, (3) $l=2$/$l=3$ alignment.
The model accounts for all three: cubic symmetry partially cancels the quadrupole, corner nodes project onto lattice planes, and both modes share the same structure.

\paragraph{Test:}
The predicted $H_0$ dipole should be \textbf{aligned with the Axis of Evil} ($l \approx 260°$, $b \approx 60°$).
A dipole orthogonal to it would disfavor a single-lattice origin.

\subsection{Dark Flow and Large-Scale Bulk Motions}
\label{sec:dark-flow}

Lattice asymmetry produces a net acceleration on the entire bubble—not just a differential expansion rate, but a coherent bulk motion.
Unlike local peculiar velocities (which average out at large scales), this externally-sourced flow is scale-independent.

Watkins et al. \cite{Watkins2023} measured a bulk flow of $419 \pm 36$ km/s at $\sim$290 Mpc using CosmicFlows-4—4.8$\sigma$ tension with $\Lambda$CDM.
Follow-up analysis \cite{Watkins2025} showed this flow is dominated by \textbf{sources external to the survey volume}—exactly what trans-observable structure predicts.
Dark flow ($l \approx 290$-$298°$), the Axis of Evil ($l \approx 260°$), and the predicted $H_0$ dipole (Section~\ref{sec:dipole}) all point to the same general sky region—expected if all trace the nearest HMEA.

The HMEA model naturally produces such flows, but we do not attempt a quantitative estimate here: the predicted bulk velocity depends on the same irregularity parameters used in the dipole estimate (Section~\ref{sec:dipole}), so it would not constitute an independent test.

\section{Observational Consistency: The ``Dark Giant'' Principle}
\label{sec:defenses}
Why don't we see objects of $\sim 10^{55}$ kg?
HMEAs are most plausibly ancient black holes ($t_{node} \gg 13.8$ Gyr) that cleared their local fuel supply long ago.
Without an accretion disk, a black hole is electromagnetically silent.
Hawking radiation is negligible: $T_H \propto 1/M$ gives $T_H \sim 10^{-83}$ K at these masses.

\section{The ``Great Metabolism'' Hypothesis}
\label{sec:metabolism}
\textit{(Speculative---not derived from simulation results.)}

The HMEA framework suggests a cyclical cosmology:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Expansion}: A node destabilizes (Big Bang), filling a void with matter—effectively a macroscopic accretion disk transporting mass across the void.
\item \textbf{Feeding}: Galaxies are stripped and accreted onto neighboring HMEAs.
\item \textbf{Destabilization}: An HMEA accumulates critical mass or merges with a neighbor.
\item \textbf{Re-Ignition}: The node detonates, initiating a new cycle.
\end{enumerate}

Our universe is a transient structure: a pulse of matter facilitating mass transfer between nodes in an eternal lattice.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The External-Node Model replaces dark energy with tidal gravity from trans-observable structure.
N-body simulations show that configurations spanning M=92-9000$\times$M$_{obs}$, S=15-38 Gpc reproduce $\Lambda$CDM expansion with $R^2 > 0.95$ (expansion rate) and $R^2 > 0.99$ (size) over 8 Gyr, using only classical physics.

Compared directly against the real Pantheon$+$SH0ES supernova data (Section~\ref{sec:from-data}), the from-simulation expansion history fits with $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof} = 0.501$ for uniform nodes at the central observer, improving to $\approx 0.44$ --- statistically indistinguishable from $\Lambda$CDM ($0.437$) --- once the nodes carry a realistic wide mass function and we score the best-matching observer rather than assuming we sit at the centre (Section~\ref{sec:hires}). This $\approx 0.44$ match is reached by \emph{many} configurations and is numerically converged to $10^5$ particles. Either way the model sits with $\Lambda$CDM and far from the Einstein--de~Sitter no-dark-energy null ($0.844$, which the data reject). The model thus produces genuine \emph{effective} dark energy, though it does not outperform $\Lambda$CDM and remains degenerate in $M/S^3$. As the traceless nature of the external tidal tensor implies (Section~\ref{sec:traceless}), the isotropic Hubble diagram cannot discriminate the structure; the model's distinguishing signal is the anisotropy it predicts.

Precisely because the Hubble diagram is such a weak discriminator, we judge configurations on a composite \emph{universe-like} criterion---supernova fit, anchored expansion, a non-collapsing core, and an empty horizon (Section~\ref{sec:universe-like}). Two results follow. First, the criterion has real selective power: choosing the node spacing on $\chi^2$ alone can favour configurations whose observable region contains a collapsing Gpc-scale core, which the joint criterion rejects. Second, once the spacing is chosen jointly, being universe-like is \emph{generic}: 68 of 72 progenitor-mass $\times$ realization configurations qualify, spanning four orders of magnitude in progenitor mass, and that verdict is stable under a fourfold increase in resolution. Treating the progenitor's mass as one parameter acting in both epochs (Section~\ref{sec:progenitor}), the theoretically distinguished value---the progenitor as a \emph{typical} node of the meta-structure---is also the most realization-robust, a convergence we did not build in.

The same geometry produces patterns consistent with three anomalies usually treated as unrelated: the Hubble Tension ($\Delta H_0/H_0 \approx 5$-$11\%$), the CMB Axis of Evil, and dark flow.
In this model, all three are consequences of a single geometric structure---and, being shear/dipole signatures rather than isotropic ones, they are precisely where the model parts ways with a pure cosmological constant.

Future work: full GR formulation, quantitative CMB multipole predictions from the lattice, and directional coherence tests using Euclid and LSST data---the anisotropy channel that the isotropic supernova fit cannot probe.
If the predicted alignment between the $H_0$ dipole, CMB multipoles, and bulk flow is confirmed, dark energy may not be an intrinsic property of spacetime, but a consequence of our position within a larger gravitational structure.
